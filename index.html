Plan the request

Knowing what to watch for after a repair is finished

A repair can look complete on the day the work ends and still leave questions that only ordinary use answers. For a Trenton home, you may want to list what was changed, what normal behaviour the provider described, and which fixtures or equipment to observe over the following weeks. Note dates and what you saw, such as a dry joint or a steady drain, so a later request can point to specific observations rather than a general worry.

  • Work completed record

    Keep the completion record that names what was repaired or replaced, so any later request can say exactly which part the work touched.

  • Described normal behaviour

    Write down how the provider said the item should behave in ordinary use, such as how a drain clears or when a pump runs.

  • Dated observations

    Jot a dated note whenever you notice something in normal use, including the time and which fixture was running.

Before you send

What to Photograph Before You Send a Request

Photos of the right parts save a lot of back-and-forth. Take these before you send the request and have them ready for the follow-up.

  • Water heater: the rating plate, the vent pipe where it leaves the tank, and the rental sticker or contract if there is one.
  • Blocked drain: which fixtures back up, whether it only happens on the lowest floor, and the floor drain or cleanout cap if you can find it.
  • Leak: the stain or damp patch, the water meter dial with every tap closed, and any recent renovation nearby.
  • Sump pump: the pit with the lid off, where the discharge pipe leaves the house, and any battery or water-powered backup.
  • Older supply pipe: the line beside the water meter. Copper is orange-brown, galvanized steel is grey with threaded joints, and lead is dull grey and shows bright silver when scratched lightly with a key.
FAQ

Questions About Your Trenton Request

What counts as watching a repair in my Trenton home?

It means observing in normal use and writing down dated notes: whether a joint stays dry, how a drain clears, how hot water behaves through your usual routine, how often a pump runs and how treated water seems at the tap. You are not asked to test or force any valve, pump or relief device.

How do I note observations after plumbing work at my Trenton address?

Keep a dated list with the date, the fixture or equipment involved, what you saw and what the household was doing at the time. Add a photograph where moisture or a mark is visible. Notes like these let a later request stay clear without relying on memory.

Does sending a Trenton request about a finished repair bind me to any work or cost?

No. Sending a request only places it in line for review by our enquiry team. Whether any provider covers your address, what they would charge and when they could attend are all unknown until a provider responds and the written scope is agreed. Nothing is booked by sending.

When should I contact the provider again after a repair is finished?

Use the completion record and the provider's own description of normal behaviour as the guide. Contact them if you see water escaping, an alarm, a repeat of the original symptom or something the record called abnormal. If you smell gas near a heater, leave and contact the gas supplier or emergency services from outside.

Can I send a Trenton request about work finished by a different provider?

Yes, you can describe work that someone else completed. Include the date it was finished, any record you were given, what you have observed since and which part you want reviewed. A responding provider will need to confirm what they can assess, and may ask for access to see the item.

Should one request cover a slow drain and a newly installed tap?

Send one request when the items may be connected, and list each observation separately with its date and location. Unrelated items, such as a repaired supply line and a softener question, are clearer as separate requests so each can be reviewed against its own completion record.
