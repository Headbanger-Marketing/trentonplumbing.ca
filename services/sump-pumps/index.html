Questions About the Work

What counts as a normal run pattern after sump pump work?

A pump that runs more or less often than it did after the work may be responding to more groundwater, or may have a fault, and dated observations help the provider judge which.

  • Get the normal pump behaviour described

    A pump and a backwater valve call for different evidence, so ask the respondent to describe how each should behave once the pit fills: when the pump should start, what it sounds like and where the water leaves. Give them the pump model, say whether a backwater valve was part of the work and photograph the discharge outlet.

  • Compare run frequency with the stated normal

    Compare how often the pump runs in your Trenton home with the pattern the provider described, and note the date and what the household was using. Do not lift the float or test the pump yourself; report what you see.

  • Record alarms, discharge and open items

    Ask for a record that names the pump, the float or alarm arrangement, the discharge line and any valve that was serviced. If an alarm sounds or water appears around the pit, tell the provider and keep a note of when; do not try to adjust the pump.
